Context

Plenty of data, no direction. The RPW homepage is where every bus operator on redPro starts their day. It showed them everything at once and prioritised nothing.

Meanwhile the levers that could actually grow their business sat on other pages entirely. Four questions went unanswered.

01

Which service needs me today?

Nothing ranked or flagged the departures at risk – operators had to scan and infer.

02

Is this number good or bad?

No competitor benchmark meant an occupancy figure carried no context.

03

So what do I do?

Even when a problem was visible, the levers to fix it – pricing, ads, ratings – lived on other pages.

04

What’s worth my attention?

Long-running shelves and promos competed with urgent, time-sensitive decisions for the same space.

The starting point

A heuristic audit of the live dashboard. It wasn’t missing data – it was missing hierarchy, context and a path to action.

Evaluated against Nielsen’s 10. Promotions sat above operations, internal jargon leaked into the UI, and “what needs me now” was scattered between an inline feed and a separate Action centre.

Each finding became a design goal: impose hierarchy, speak the operator’s language, benchmark every metric, surface the day’s priority first, unify the action vocabulary, and put the fix one tap from the problem.

Discovery

Three reads that set the direction. From stakeholder feedback and three months of intervention data.

01 · Timing

At any moment, an operator cares most about services departing today (DBD 0) and tomorrow (DBD 1) – not last month’s averages. This anchored the page on a two-tab, today-first tracker.

02 · Mental model

Operators triage by the service at risk, ranked worst-first – they don’t read a dashboard top to bottom. Services got ranked by lowest occupancy; everything healthy got demoted.

03 · Context & action

A metric with no benchmark and no next step is noise. Operators wanted “is this good or bad – and what do I do?” Every diagnosis got a benchmark and a recommended fix.

Strategy

The bet: don’t show more – show what to do. The brief read like a data gap. It was a hierarchy and action gap.

The reframe. Obvious read: show every metric and let operators find what matters. The bet: surface only the at-risk services, benchmarked, each with one recommended fix. Demote everything else – promos included.

01

Lead with what’s wrong

Triage before overview – the first thing on screen is the service that needs action today.

02

Never a number alone

Every metric ships with a competitor benchmark. Context is the product, not the raw value.

03

Every problem, a fix

A flag without an explainable, recommended action is just anxiety – pair each with a next step.

04

Urgent over evergreen

Time-sensitive decisions earn the top; promos and shelves move below the fold.

Explorations

How the recommendations get decided. A rules-based, explainable matrix over a generic “recommended for you” feed – operators only trust a nudge they can see the reason for.

Option A · rejected

Show everything

Bet: more metrics, more control for power users. Why not: the status quo – no hierarchy, no action. The very problem we started with.

Option B · rejected

Generic “for you” feed

Bet: a black-box model ranks what to promote. Why not: unexplainable – operators can’t see why they’re being sold something, and trust erodes fast.

✓ Option C · chosen

Rules matrix

Bet: recommendations tied to two readable axes. Result: every card is explainable from the data on screen – occupancy × search rank.

The matrix. Occupancy × SRP rank (search visibility) – every recommendation traces back to one of four cases.

Solution

Each part answers one operator question. Not a screen gallery – the worst-performing departure is always the first thing an operator sees.

01

“Which service needs me today?” – the DBD 0/1 tracker leads

Two tabs, today and tomorrow, with services ranked worst-occupancy first. The at-risk departure comes before any promo or shelf.

02

“Is this good or bad?” – benchmarked, not raw

Each service is diagnosed against competitors – occupancy, selling-price range, SRP-rank bucket and rating – with a single “compare with competitors” path.

03

“So what do I do?” – a recommended fix, with its upside

Every flag carries one action from the matrix, stating its projected upside before the operator commits – “up to 50 ranks,” a 15% discount, 45 sponsored days – pre-filled, never recommending what’s already configured.

04

“What’s worth my attention?” – urgent on top, “For You” below

A performance trend lets operators zoom out; the “For You” shelf moves to the bottom, present but never competing with the day’s priorities.

Validation

From reporting to acting. Every recommendation traces back to a heuristic it fixes, so the argument holds before a single metric lands.

The homepage becomes the front door for monetisation products – surfacing Rise and revMax lead-gen exactly when a service’s data makes the case. Benchmarked diagnosis turns raw metrics into decisions an operator can act on.

The hardest part

Designing the recommendation matrix so it never felt arbitrary. Every card had to be explainable – tied to occupancy and rank – and had to carry an honest projection, or operators would stop trusting it.

What’s next

Closing the loop – showing operators the realised impact after they act on a recommendation, so the homepage learns to earn the next tap.
