Case study · redBus / Bitla

Invoice reconciliation tool

Operators didn’t need a cleaner table of mismatches – they needed to know whether a difference was worth their time. I redesigned the flow around that, tested two directions head-to-head with real operators, and shipped the one that made the “why” visible without a click.

Reframe the question – from “what” to “why”

Two in-person operator interviews turned a screen-level brief into a product-level reframe: operators don’t want a cleaner mismatch table, they want to know whether a difference is worth their time.

Cut the friction operators never asked to keep

Two manual uploads became an auto-reconciliation flow, syncing directly from redPro via API – removing the exact step operators complained about, unprompted, in research.

Earn trust with rules, not just polish

The AI Summary layer only states what it can prove – a rule that exists because I caught it lying to me first, in my own first draft.

How it came together

01 · Research

Two operators, one afternoon each

I took a printed questionnaire on the road to Seabird Tourists and Rajesh Transports, expecting to validate a screen. Neither described wanting a cleaner table – both described wanting to be told why. Rajesh’s whole answer to “what does resolved mean to you” was one word: “Justification.”

02 · Redesign

Auto-sync, not two manual uploads

Bitla GDS now pulls data straight from redPro via API, and the bank statement upload becomes optional rather than blocking. Non-redBus OTAs still need a manual upload – an open question I flagged rather than resolved unilaterally.

03 · Testing

Two real layouts, tested head-to-head

Comprehension questions with known answers, timed tasks, and one that cut past politeness: “Would you send a dispute to redBus based only on this?” V1 won on data visualisation – its charts made the “why” visible without a click.

The AI Summary, and the rules that govern it

On top of the detailed tables, the AI Summary answers the operator’s real question – fine or not fine, and what kind of not-fine – in one read, before they touch a table.

  1. 01

    Lead with the state

    “97% reconciled,” not “33 mismatches found.” Opening with the problem count turns every invoice into a perceived crisis.

  2. 02

    Never overclaim confidence

    Caught a real bug where the category breakdown didn’t sum to the headline number. Now it only states what it can trace to a real value.

  3. 03

    Traceable, not just readable

    Every claim is one click from its source – clicking “33 PNRs” filters straight to those rows.

  4. 04

    Scoped to where it helps

    The escalation screen gets a terse one-line version, so it never competes with the audit trail for attention.

More from this flow

Built in BITLA’s real production tokens – what operators tested is what could ship as-is.

Case study · redBus / redPro

HubbleUI design system

PARTICLES looked like a design system. Nothing in production actually used it. I rebuilt its color foundation from measured, provable drift – then migrated redPro’s first real component onto it, found a shipped accessibility bug on the way, and built the tooling to stop the drift from ever coming back unwatched.

Audit before engineering

Every family’s hue, saturation and lightness measured and plotted on a hue wheel – turning “these look a bit off” into a provable, fixable fact.

Lock hue – without erasing character

One precise hue per family, but saturation and lightness hand-tuned. A fully normalized version produced an acidic green and muddy ambers, so I rejected it.

Build the thing that watches

A rebuilt system still drifts the moment nobody’s watching – so the work ends in a Figma plugin, not just a cleaner file.

How it came together

01 · Gray scale

12 scattered values, 9 engineered stops

One locked 216° undertone and lightness steps that rise unevenly, because vision compresses dark tones. Old gray-300, 400 and 500 all sat near 81% – one color wearing three names.

02 · Buttons migration

Finding the real bug

Disabled + Hover rendered white text on light gray. The root cause: Disabled lived in the color-intent axis. Making it a state made the whole bug class impossible.

03 · The tool

HubbleUI Design Assistant

A Figma plugin I built: audit + auto-fix, a weighted Adoption Score, a detector for three kinds of drift, and an AI fidelity check against real builds.

Four ways the plugin catches drift

Auditing 27 more components by hand doesn’t scale. Each feature closes a different way drift actually happens.

  1. 01

    Audit + auto-fix

    Off-system values get a severity, a plain-language reason, and the nearest token – by colour-distance matching.

  2. 02

    Adoption Score

    One number, five weighted dimensions – something a lead can track file over file.

  3. 03

    Detached Instance Detector

    Detached, broken-link and overridden instances – each override tagged token-bound or uncontrolled.

  4. 04

    DAT – AI fidelity check

    Claude compares a dev build to the Figma design with a fixed rubric and returns a scored report.

Case study · redBus / redPro v3 · RPW

redPro homepage dashboard

The old dashboard was a wall of metrics – operators could read a hundred numbers but not tell which service was in trouble, or what to do about it. I turned it into a to-do list.

Team

Surface
Operator home dashboard · redPro v3
Users
Bus operators
Status
Shipped · 2025

Timing

Operators care most about services departing today and tomorrow – not last month’s averages. The page is anchored on a today-first tracker.

Mental model

Operators triage by the service at risk, ranked worst-first. Services are ranked by lowest occupancy; everything healthy is demoted.

Context & action

A metric with no benchmark and no next step is noise. Every diagnosis is paired with a competitor benchmark and a recommended fix.

How it came together

01 · Heuristic audit

Missing hierarchy, not data

Evaluated against Nielsen’s 10: promos above operations, jargon like “270 PAX impacted,” numbers with no benchmark, and six different ways to say “act.”

Empty & unseenFill seats & get found
Empty but visibleWin on price
Full but unseenBuy visibility
Full & visibleNo action needed

02 · The rules matrix

Explainable, not a black box

Every recommendation traces back to occupancy × search rank. A generic “for you” feed was rejected – operators only trust a nudge they can see the reason for.

03 · Solution

Each part answers one question

The DBD 0/1 tracker leads, each service is benchmarked against competitors, every flag carries a pre-filled fix with its projected upside, and “For You” moves below.

The bet: don’t show more – show what to do

The brief read like a data gap. It was a hierarchy and action gap.

  1. 01

    Lead with what’s wrong

    Triage before overview – the first thing on screen is the service that needs action today.

  2. 02

    Never a number alone

    Every metric ships with a competitor benchmark. Context is the product.

  3. 03

    Every problem, a fix

    A flag without an explainable, recommended action is just anxiety.

  4. 04

    Urgent over evergreen

    Time-sensitive decisions earn the top; promos and shelves move below the fold.

Before and after

From a wall of metrics to a homepage that tells operators what to do next.

  • 50–80% · watch medium
  • > 80% · healthy high
